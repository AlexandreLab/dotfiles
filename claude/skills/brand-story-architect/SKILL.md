# B2C Brand Story Architect

Facilitation framework for building resonant B2C brand stories. Conducts a structured discovery interview across 5 pillars, then runs a dead-angle diagnostic to surface blind spots.

## How to Run a Session

### 1. Open

Anchor the conversation before asking anything:

> "Let's build your brand story from the consumer up – not the product down. I'll ask questions across five areas, then flag every gap I find. Some questions will feel uncomfortable. That's intentional."

### 2. Discovery Interview

Work through each pillar in order. If the founder's earlier answers already address a question, note it and move on – don't repeat.

---

#### Pillar 1 – Hero & Villain
The consumer is the hero. The brand is the tool. Define the conflict.

- Describe your customer's life *before* they find you. What is their daily frustration?
- Who or what is the **villain** – the status quo, old technology, or broken system they are fighting?
- What does life look like *after* your product? What transformation happens?
- Can you name the villain out loud in your messaging, or does it feel too risky?

---

#### Pillar 2 – Brand Identity
Brands that connect feel like people, not companies.

- If your brand showed up at a dinner party, how would they behave? Describe in 3 words.
- What is the raw, unpolished origin story – the "little dirt" version you haven't told publicly?
- What opinion does your brand hold that would alienate 20% of people? *(If the answer is "none," flag this as a dead angle.)*
- What would your brand **never** say, even under pressure?

---

#### Pillar 3 – Mission Beyond Transaction
Cult-like followings are built around movements, not products.

- Beyond the product itself, what fundamental human desire are you fulfilling? *(Options: belonging, status, peace of mind, self-expression, freedom, fear of missing out, safety – pick the truest one.)*
- What would be lost in the world if your brand shut down tomorrow?
- What larger societal challenge or better future are you fighting for?
- Is your mission something customers would tattoo on themselves – or just something they'd click "like" on?

---

#### Pillar 4 – 11-Star Experience
Viral word-of-mouth comes from experiences that defy expectation.

- What does a standard 5-star customer experience look like for you today?
- Imagine an impossible, physics-defying 11-star experience – Elon Musk picks you up at the airport to take you to space. Describe yours.
- Working backward from impossible: what is one specific, executable 6 or 7-star gesture that would make a customer tell 5 friends unprompted?

---

#### Pillar 5 – Brand Bravery
In crowded markets, safe is invisible.

- What industry convention or norm does your brand actively reject?
- When a customer uses or wears or shares your product, what does it say about them to the world? What identity does it signal?
- Describe a campaign idea that would make your PR team or investors nervous. *(If nothing comes to mind, flag this.)*

---

### 3. Dead Angle Diagnostics

After gathering answers, score against these 9 failure patterns. Flag every dead angle found – with severity.

| Dead Angle | Symptom | Probe If Unclear |
|---|---|---|
| **The "we" trap** | Story centers on the brand, not the customer | "Who is the hero in your About page – you or your customer?" |
| **No villain** | No enemy or obstacle; narrative feels flat | "What are you fighting *against*, not just for?" |
| **Generic aspiration** | Mission too vague: "make people happy", "change the world" | "What specifically would be lost if you shut down?" |
| **Polished insincerity** | No raw truth, no imperfection, too corporate | "What's the embarrassing origin story you haven't told publicly?" |
| **Feature focus** | Describes capabilities, not transformation | "What does your customer's day *feel like* after vs. before?" |
| **Invisible tribe** | No community, no shared identity, no belonging | "Who are your customers to each other?" |
| **Same-old 5-star** | Meeting expectations, nothing memorable | "What would make a customer text a friend about you tonight?" |
| **Safe messaging** | No opinion held, no one alienated, no one inspired | "Who is your product *not* for, and does that bother you?" |
| **No cultural currency** | Product doesn't let customers express their identity | "What does owning/using your product say about a person?" |

**Severity:**
- **Critical** – the story will not land without fixing this
- **Notable** – weakens resonance, addressable in messaging
- **Watch** – not broken yet, but likely to surface at scale

---

### 4. Synthesis Output

Produce a Brand Story Brief after the full interview:

```
## Brand Story Brief

**The Hero:** [who the customer is and where they start]
**The Villain:** [the enemy / status quo they face]
**The Transformation:** [the life change the product enables]
**The Human Mission:** [the deeper desire being fulfilled]
**The Brand Personality:** [3 words + the raw truth]
**The Brave Opinion:** [what the brand stands for that not everyone agrees with]
**The 6-Star Gesture:** [one specific, executable memorable experience]
**Cultural Currency:** [what using this product says about a person]

## Dead Angles Found
[list each gap: name / severity / recommended fix]
```

After presenting the brief, ask:
> "Would you like to drill into any of these? The areas with the highest leverage are usually the villain definition and the brave opinion – most founders underinvest in both."

---

## Common Mistakes

**Skipping the villain.** Founders want to be positive. A villain creates the tension that gives the hero's journey meaning. No villain = no story, just features.

**Confusing brand personality with brand voice.** Personality is who you are; voice is how you speak. Nail personality first. Voice follows.

**Picking the safe human desire.** Most brands default to "community." Push harder. The real driver is usually status, fear of irrelevance, or freedom from something specific.

**Leaving 11-star as a thought experiment.** It must produce one specific, executable 6-star move. Push until a concrete gesture emerges – a name, a room, a specific object.

**Accepting the polished answer.** First answers are often the public-facing version. The second or third answer, after pushback, is usually the real one.

**One round of questions.** Brand story is iterative. The first answers reveal what second-layer questions to ask. Plan for at least one loop.
